# RMBLR — first launch film

## The product
An open-source (MIT) Android app. A small orb appears **only when a text field has the
cursor** — never on your home screen. Tap it and it dictates: the transcript is written
straight into the box you were typing in, in any app. Hold it and an arc of tones fans
out; slide to one, release, speak. Flick straight at one and it fires that tone
immediately. Double tap and it translates instead — say it in English, it lands in
Japanese or isiZulu, inside WhatsApp. Drag it and it stays put.

The tones on the arc change with the app you are in. Your own API key, no account, no
subscription.

## The one directorial idea — "One thumb, four verbs"
The subject is a GESTURE, not a screen. So the camera never leaves the orb. The orb stays
pinned at one position and the apps change BEHIND it. The same thumb arrives at the same
orb four times and each touch is a different verb with a different outcome. The film is a
gesture vocabulary, demonstrated — legible by repetition with variation, not by narration.

Cold open: a caret blinks in an empty text field, and the orb appears *because* the caret
appeared. Cause and effect in one shot.

Hero beat: hold the orb in a chat app — the arc reads Casual / Clean up / Bullets. Whip to
a mail app, hold again — the same arc now reads Professional / Summary. One gesture, two
answers, no settings screen.

## Non-negotiables
- SHOW THE ACTUAL PRODUCT. The real UI, recreated as close to 1:1 as possible from the
  real Kotlin sources and the real colour tokens in `app/.../ui/theme/Color.kt`. Not
  generic phone mockups.
- FOLLOW THE THUMB. When the interaction happens at the orb, the camera is at the orb and
  you see the touch arrive. No cutting between disconnected feature slides.
- No "X, not Y" antithesis. No "Most apps…". State it flat.
- Nothing half-built on screen: no snippet shortcuts, no sound/keyboard-theme prefs.
  Everything shown must be true of the shipped APK.

## What to lead on (honest differentiators)
It appears only when you are actually typing · your own API key, no account, no
subscription · the tones change with the app you are in · a dictionary of your own words ·
open source, MIT.
